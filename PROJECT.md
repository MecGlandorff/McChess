# Project Scope

McChess studies compact neural chess trained from human games, without engine
supervision. The goal is a reproducible research platform for board
representation, policy/value learning, and search under limited compute.

The current system processes PGNs, trains single-board ResNets, and evaluates
policy-only and MCTS bots. History, attention, search distillation, optional
self-play, and a project-defined NNUE-style model are future studies, not
implemented capabilities. [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) tracks those boundaries.

## Supervision And Non-Goals

Allowed targets are human moves, final human-game results, McChess self-play
results, and visit distributions generated by McChess's own MCTS.

Stockfish may be an external evaluation opponent only. Its moves, evaluations,
and game outcomes must not become training labels, distillation targets,
checkpoint-selection criteria, or hyperparameter-tuning targets. Leela labels,
Syzygy or other tablebases, external best-move labels, and imported engine
weights are excluded.

`python-chess` supplies game rules and legal moves; explicit legal masking is
mandatory. The project does not aim to beat Stockfish, maximize an online
rating, or require large-scale compute.

## Questions To Test

| Question | Comparison and measurements |
|---|---|
| What does human-game supervision teach a compact model? | Policy-only and policy/value baselines; legal top-1/3/5, value MSE and calibration, baseline arena score. |
| Does temporal context help? | Single-board, stacked-history, and board-sequence inputs; matched data, budget, and seed. |
| Which architecture uses limited compute best? | ResNet, history, attention, and optional sparse accumulator; accuracy, parameter count, training and inference speed. |
| Which models benefit from search? | Policy-only and MCTS-25/50/100/400; W/D/L, game length, simulations and distinct evaluations per second, score under fixed time. |
| Can search improve policy without engine labels? | Human targets, McChess visit distributions, and hybrid targets; policy accuracy, KL divergence, and before/after arena score. |
| Where does play fail? | Hanging pieces, missed mate in one, unsafe captures, king safety, endgame conversion, repetition/draw handling, and tactical horizon limits. |

Architecture comparisons require matched datasets, seeds, training budgets,
openings, and search settings. Report speed alongside quality; a slower model's
higher fixed-simulation score alone does not establish better use of compute.

## Evidence And Success

Establish chess-rule and tensor correctness first, then data-pipeline and tiny
overfit checks, saved supervised metrics, baseline arenas, matched MCTS
comparisons, and finally architecture or training ablations. Implementation
alone is not evidence of playing strength.

Report measured outcomes under named configs and checkpoint identifiers.
Preserve failed, negative, and inconclusive findings. Results belong in
[RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md) with supporting reports; run metadata requirements are
in [REPRODUCIBILITY.md](REPRODUCIBILITY.md) and the
[evaluation protocol](docs/EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md).

Success means correct contracts, clear implementation boundaries, reproducible
runs, useful baselines, and honest limits. Unsupported Elo or modern-engine
comparisons are not success criteria.
